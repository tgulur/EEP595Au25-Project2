Deep Learning and Voltage Fingerprinting for Advanced Intrusion Detection on Automotive CAN Networks
Tejas Gulur
Keerthi Pobba
Bryan Gonzalez
Introduction
As vehicles become increasingly computerized, securing their in-vehicle networks is critical for both safety and privacy. The Controller Area Network (CAN) bus, despite being essential for automotive communication, was not designed with strong security in mind. As a result, attacks that inject forged messages, disrupt normal communication, or impersonate legitimate Electronic Control Units (ECUs) have become real threats. While traditional detection methods focus primarily on timing analysis, skilled adversaries are finding ways to evade these systems.
Objectives
This project aims to design and evaluate an advanced intrusion detection system (IDS) for CAN networks by integrating two promising approaches:
Voltage fingerprinting: By analyzing the subtle, device-specific voltage signatures of CAN messages, the IDS can identify the true source of a message, providing a physical-layer barrier against spoofing attempts.
Deep learning-based analyzers: Modern neural networks, such as Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks, will be trained to recognize complex and previously unseen attack patterns by learning from large-scale, realistic datasets.
Signal Integration Layer: Develop a decision level integration layer that merges voltage fingerprinting and deep learning signals into a single score with a unified threshold and alert policy. Evaluates a stacking combiner of both scores, confidences, and short term trends to learn an adaptive weighting.
Approach
We will leverage open-access datasets, including the CAN Bus Voltage Dataset and the ROAD CAN IDS Dataset, to ensure both physical signal traces and annotated attack traffic are available for experimentation. The project will develop, train, and rigorously evaluate both voltage-based and deep learning IDS components in isolation and as a hybrid architecture. The system will be validated against a variety of realistic attack scenarios, from common denial-of-service and fuzzing to advanced spoofing and cloaking.
Significance and Impact
This research will help address a clear need in automotive cybersecurity for defenses that work at both the physical and data layers. By combining voltage fingerprinting and deep learning, the proposed IDS will set a new benchmark for detection accuracy and resilience, even as attacks grow more sophisticated.

Risks & Mitigation
Dataset Limitations: If public datasets lack attack diversity, augment via CAN simulation tools and synthetic attacks.
Model Overfitting: Employ cross-validation and adversarial/noise robustness testing.
